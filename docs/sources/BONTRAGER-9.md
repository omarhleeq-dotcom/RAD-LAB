## General (identification, markers, display)
- **p.32** — Every image needs patient identification and an anatomic side marker. The R / L marker indicates the patient's right or left side; it is placed on the image receptor inside the lateral portion of the collimated border of the side identified, within the field and not over essential anatomy. A digital side annotation added after the exposure is not acceptable (the image is repeated).
- **p.33** — An "upright" / "erect" marker distinguishes erect chest / abdomen images from recumbent ones; a decubitus marker is used for decubitus images; INSP / EXP markers are used on comparison PA chests.
- **p.36** — PA and AP chests are displayed with the patient's right on the viewer's left. **LATERAL** positions are marked R or L by the **side of the patient closest to the image receptor**; the display orientation of laterals varies by department.
- **p.81** — Chest kV is high enough to give mid-grey lungs: 110–125 kV; above 100 kV generally with a grid.
- **p.82** — Centre to **T7** for most chest projections (T7 is at the inferior angle of the scapula); for AP, T7 is about 8–10 cm below the jugular notch. Expose on the second full inspiration; 10 posterior ribs shown on inspiration. Arm supports can keep the arms raised for the lateral in older patients.

## Chest PA
- **p.83** — Rotation is judged by the sternal clavicle ends being equidistant from the spine; the sternal clavicle end **closest to the spine** indicates the side rotated **toward the image receptor** (e.g. LAO). Erect is preferred; PA at **183 cm** minimises heart magnification.
- **p.92 (PA, ambulatory)** — SID minimum 183 cm; 35 × 43 cm image receptor, portrait or landscape; grid; 110–125 kV; shoulders forward and down; central ray to T7 (18–20 cm below the vertebra prominens or at the inferior angle of the scapula); second full inspiration. Criteria include at least 10 posterior ribs, sternoclavicular joints equidistant, scapulae clear of the lungs.

## Chest LATERAL
- **p.84** — **Left lateral** unless the protocol or right-sided pathology calls for the right. **True lateral:** standing straight, weight even on both feet, arms raised, posterior shoulders and pelvis superimposed; chin raised. Posterior rib separation caused by beam divergence at 183 cm is about **1 cm** (¼–½ in); more than that indicates rotation.
- **p.94 (lateral, ambulatory)** — SID minimum 183 cm; 35 × 43 cm receptor, portrait; grid; 110–125 kV; erect, **left side against the receptor** unless the complaint is right-sided; weight even on both feet; **arms raised above the head**, chin up; midsagittal plane parallel to the receptor; central ray perpendicular at **mid-thorax, T7** (7.5–10 cm below the jugular notch); in broad-shouldered patients lower the central ray / receptor at least 2.5 cm from the PA level; second full inspiration. Criteria: apices to costophrenic angles included, sternum to posterior ribs, far-side posterior ribs / costophrenic angle about 1 cm posterior, hilum near the receptor centre.

## Chest AP
- **p.87** — AP chest: central ray 8–10 cm below the jugular notch; landscape receptor recommended for most AP chests; upper collimation to the vertebra prominens.
- **p.96 (AP supine / semi-erect)** — SID minimum 183 cm, or as possible for semi-erect; grid strongly recommended; 110–125 kV; shoulders rolled forward by internal arm rotation; central ray about **5° caudad** (perpendicular to the sternum) to T7, 8–10 cm below the jugular notch; label the SID and "AP supine" / "AP semi-erect". Criteria as for the PA except that the heart is larger, often 8–9 posterior ribs are seen, and the clavicles are horizontal with the apices clear.
