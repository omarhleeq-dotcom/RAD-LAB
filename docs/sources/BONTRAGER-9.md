## General (identification, markers, display)
- **p.32** — Every image needs patient identification and an anatomic side marker. The R / L marker indicates the patient's right or left side; it is placed on the image receptor inside the lateral portion of the collimated border of the side identified, within the field and not over essential anatomy. A digital side annotation added after the exposure is not acceptable (the image is repeated).
- **p.33** — An "upright" / "erect" marker distinguishes erect chest / abdomen images from recumbent ones; a decubitus marker is used for decubitus images; INSP / EXP markers are used on comparison PA chests.
- **p.36** — PA and AP chests are displayed with the patient's right on the viewer's left. **LATERAL** positions are marked R or L by the **side of the patient closest to the image receptor**; the display orientation of laterals varies by department.
- **p.81** — Chest kV is high enough to give mid-grey lungs: 110–125 kV; above 100 kV generally with a grid. *(Bontrager gives only this optimal band; the engine's 100–109 / 126–150 partial-credit bands are an engine teaching choice, S12-1, not Bontrager.)*
- **p.82** — Centre to **T7** for most chest projections (T7 is at the inferior angle of the scapula); for AP, T7 is about 8–10 cm below the jugular notch. Expose on the second full inspiration; 10 posterior ribs shown on inspiration. Arm supports can keep the arms raised for the lateral in older patients.

## Chest PA
- **p.83** — Rotation is judged by the sternal clavicle ends being equidistant from the spine; the sternal clavicle end **closest to the spine** indicates the side rotated **toward the image receptor** (e.g. LAO). Erect is preferred; PA at **183 cm** minimises heart magnification.
- **p.92 (PA, ambulatory)** — SID **minimum** 183 cm (Bontrager states no upper limit or partial band; the engine's 185 cm upper full-credit limit and 150–200 cm partial band are engine teaching choices, S12-1); 35 × 43 cm image receptor, portrait or landscape; grid; 110–125 kV; shoulders forward and down; central ray to T7 (18–20 cm below the vertebra prominens or at the inferior angle of the scapula); second full inspiration. Criteria include at least 10 posterior ribs, sternoclavicular joints equidistant, scapulae clear of the lungs.

## Chest LATERAL
- **p.84** — **Left lateral** unless the protocol or right-sided pathology calls for the right. **True lateral:** standing straight, weight even on both feet, arms raised, posterior shoulders and pelvis superimposed; chin raised. Posterior rib separation caused by beam divergence at 183 cm is about **1 cm** (¼–½ in); more than that indicates rotation.
- **p.94 (lateral, ambulatory)** — SID minimum 183 cm; 35 × 43 cm receptor, portrait; grid; 110–125 kV; erect, **left side against the receptor** unless the complaint is right-sided; weight even on both feet; **arms raised above the head**, chin up; midsagittal plane parallel to the receptor; central ray perpendicular at **mid-thorax, T7** (7.5–10 cm below the jugular notch); in broad-shouldered patients lower the central ray / receptor at least 2.5 cm from the PA level; second full inspiration. Criteria: apices to costophrenic angles included, sternum to posterior ribs, far-side posterior ribs / costophrenic angle about 1 cm posterior, hilum near the receptor centre.

## Chest AP
- **p.87** — AP chest: central ray 8–10 cm below the jugular notch; landscape receptor recommended for most AP chests; upper collimation to the vertebra prominens.
- **p.96 (AP supine / semi-erect)** — SID minimum 183 cm, or as possible for semi-erect; grid strongly recommended; 110–125 kV; shoulders rolled forward by internal arm rotation; central ray about **5° caudad** (perpendicular to the sternum) to T7, 8–10 cm below the jugular notch; label the SID and "AP supine" / "AP semi-erect". Criteria as for the PA except that the heart is larger, often 8–9 posterior ribs are seen, and the clavicles are horizontal with the apices clear.

## Wrist (S14.1a; claims supplied by the owner's advisor in the S14.1 prompt, verified by the advisor against the owner's PDF; PDF page = printed page + 20; not re-verified by Claude Code)
- **p.156 (PA wrist) and p.158 (lateral wrist), common:** patient seated at the end of the table, shoulder, elbow and wrist on the same horizontal plane; minimum SID 40 in (102 cm, conversion); image receptor 18 × 24 cm, portrait, the smallest available, collimated to the area of interest; non-grid, detail screens; shield radiosensitive tissues outside the region of interest; central ray perpendicular to the receptor, directed to the midcarpal area; collimate on all four sides to include the distal radius and ulna and the mid-metacarpal area; hand / wrist long axis aligned with and centred on the receptor long axis; no motion. Exposure criteria: optimal density and contrast, sharp bony trabecular markings and visible soft-tissue margins (fat pads).
- **p.156 (PA):** hand pronated, hand arched slightly so the wrist / carpals contact the receptor closely; digital **60 ± 5 kV** (analog 60–70). True PA is shown by equal concavity on both sides of the proximal metacarpal shafts, near-equal distances between the proximal metacarpals, and the distal radius and ulna separated (only minimal superimposition at the distal radioulnar joint). Not every intercarpal space is open (normal overlap).
- **p.158 (lateral):** thumb-up lateral, fingers comfortably extended; a radiolucent support block and a sandbag are allowed; digital **65 ± 5 kV** (analog 60–70). True lateral is shown by the ulnar head superimposed over the distal radius and the proximal 2nd–5th metacarpals superimposed / aligned. Anatomy shown: distal radius / ulna, carpals, at least the mid-metacarpal area.
- **Bontrager is silent (as supplied) on:** mAs, AEC, any rotation tolerance, any SID upper bound, a numeric collimation margin, the viewer-side display of a single-limb marker. These are engine teaching choices or owner decisions (`RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Wrist.md` §3–§5; DECISIONS S14.1-4, O21–O26).

## Hand (S14.2a; claims supplied by the owner's advisor, verified by the advisor against the owner's PDF; PDF page = printed page + 20; "Cp joint" in the PDF text extraction = MCP joint; not re-verified by Claude Code)
- **p.150 (PA hand) and p.152 (fan lateral hand), common:** minimum SID 40 in (102 cm, conversion); non-grid, detail screens; image receptor 24 × 30 cm portrait, the smallest available, collimated to the area of interest; shield radiosensitive tissue outside the region of interest; patient seated at the end of the table, hand and forearm extended, long axis of hand / forearm aligned with the long axis of the receptor; central ray perpendicular; collimate on four sides to the outer margins of the hand and wrist; anatomy shown = entire hand and wrist plus about 2.5 cm (1 in) of distal forearm.
- **p.150 (PA):** analog 60–70 kV, digital **60 ± 5 kV**. Hand pronated, palm on the receptor, fingers slightly spread; hand and wrist centred; CR to the **third MCP joint**. Criteria: symmetric concavities of the metacarpal shafts and phalanges of digits 2–5, equal soft tissue on each side of phalanges 2–5; digits slightly separated, soft tissues not overlapping; MCP and IP joints open; thumb appears oblique (expected); CR / collimation centred at the third MCP joint. If both hands are requested, expose them separately.
- **p.152 (fan lateral, preferred when the phalanges are the area of interest):** analog 60–70 kV, digital **65 ± 5 kV**; a 45° foam step support is listed; a compensation filter may be used (thickness differences). Hand rotated to lateral, thumb side up; fingers and thumb spread in a fan on radiolucent blocks, digits separated and parallel to the receptor; metacarpals not rotated; CR to the **second MCP joint**. Criteria: fingers equally separated; phalanges in true lateral with open joint spaces; thumb slightly oblique, free of superimposition, joints open; distal radius / ulna superimposed and metacarpals superimposed; CR / collimation centred at the second MCP joint.
- **p.153 (lateral in extension / flexion, alternatives; also used for foreign-body localisation and displaced metacarpal fractures; flexion may be less painful):** same kV as the fan lateral; thumb side up; second–fifth MCP joints centred; CR to the second–fifth MCP joints. Extension: fingers / thumb extended against a support block, fingers and metacarpals superimposed. Flexion: fingers in natural flexion, thumb lightly touching the first finger. Criteria: distal radius / ulna superimposed; metacarpals and phalanges superimposed; thumb slightly oblique, free of superimposition, joints open.
- **Bontrager is silent (as supplied) on:** mAs, AEC, any rotation tolerance, partial kV bands, any SID upper bound, numeric collimation margin or centring tolerance, forearm-coverage tolerance, the viewer-side display of a single-limb marker. These are engine teaching choices or owner decisions (`RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Hand.md` §3–§5, §9; DECISIONS S14.2-1 … S14.2-5).

## Elbow (S14.2b phase 2; claims supplied by the owner's advisor, verified by the advisor against the owner's PDF; PDF page = printed page + 20; not re-verified by Claude Code)
- **p.166 (AP elbow, fully extended) and p.172 (lateromedial elbow), common:** minimum SID 40 in (102 cm, conversion); non-grid, detail screens; image receptor 24 × 30 cm, the smallest available (AP: landscape for two exposures or portrait for one; lateral: portrait), collimated on four sides to the area of interest; shield radiosensitive tissue outside the region of interest; patient seated at the end of the table.
- **p.166 (AP extended):** analog 65–75 kV, digital **70 ± 5 kV**. Elbow fully extended if possible, hand supinated, arm and forearm aligned with the receptor long axis, elbow joint centred, patient leans laterally as needed; palpate the epicondyles so the interepicondylar plane is parallel to the receptor; CR perpendicular to the mid-elbow joint, about 2 cm distal to the midpoint of the line between the epicondyles. Criteria: distal humerus, joint space and proximal radius and ulna visible; no rotation = epicondyles in profile and radial head, neck and tubercle separated or only slightly superimposed by the ulna; olecranon seated in the fossa in full extension; joint space open; CR and collimation centred at the mid-elbow. Acute / partial flexion AP variants exist on p.167–168 (not built).
- **p.172 (lateromedial):** analog 60–70 kV, digital **70 ± 5 kV**. Elbow flexed 90°, forearm aligned with the receptor long axis, elbow joint centred, shoulder dropped so humerus and forearm lie in one horizontal plane; hand / wrist in true lateral, thumb side up, interepicondylar plane perpendicular to the receptor; a support under the hand / wrist for a heavy forearm; CR perpendicular to the mid-elbow, about 4 cm medial to the palpable posterior surface of the olecranon. Criteria: forearm aligned, elbow flexed 90°; about half of the radial head superimposed by the coronoid process, olecranon in profile; true lateral = three concentric arcs of the trochlear sulcus, double ridges of the capitulum and trochlea and the trochlear notch, epicondyles superimposed; CR / collimation centre at the mid-elbow; anterior and posterior fat pads visible (90° flexion is needed to show the posterior fat pad; 30–35° only when specifically indicated).
- **Bontrager is silent (as supplied) on:** mAs, AEC, any rotation tolerance, partial kV bands, any SID upper bound, numeric collimation margin or centring tolerance, the viewer-side display of a single-limb marker (`RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Elbow.md` §0).

## Knee and foot (S14.4 Phase A; claims read by the owner from Bontrager 9e chapter 6 and supplied in the S14.4 prompt; PDF page = printed page + 20; not re-verified by Claude Code)
- **p.224–225, lower-limb common:** minimum SID 40 in (102 cm, conversion); low-to-medium kV, short exposure time, small focal spot; digital overall 70–85 kV range for the lower limb; parts distal to the knee are non-grid, a grid is used when the part is > 10 cm (average knee 9–13 cm, either may be used; this text: non-grid ≤ 10 cm, grid > 10 cm, especially the AP knee); four-sided collimation preferred; gonadal shielding good practice; R / L marker on the lateral margin of the collimation field without covering anatomy.
- **p.232 (foot AP / dorsoplantar):** receptor 24 × 30 cm portrait, non-grid; digital 65 ± 5 kV (analog 60 ± 5); supine, knee flexed, sole flat on the receptor, long axis of the foot aligned with the CR and the receptor portion; CR angled 10° toward the heel (perpendicular to the metatarsals; high arch about 15°, low arch about 5°; no angle for a foreign body) to the base of the 3rd metatarsal; collimate to the foot margins on four sides. Criteria: whole foot (all phalanges, metatarsals, navicular, cuneiforms, cuboid); no rotation = nearly equal spacing of metatarsals 2–5; bases of the 1st / 2nd metatarsals generally separated, bases 2–5 overlapping; joint between the 1st and 2nd cuneiforms shown; sesamoids seen through the 1st metatarsal head; collimation to the area of interest.
- **p.233 (foot AP medial oblique):** same technical factors; CR perpendicular to the receptor, base of the 3rd metatarsal; foot rotated medially so the plantar surface is 30°–40° to the receptor plane (the text recommends 40°, dorsum parallel to the receptor); collimate to the skin margins on four sides. Criteria: entire foot from the distal phalanges to the posterior calcaneus and proximal talus; metatarsals 3–5 free of superimposition, 1st / 2nd free except at the bases; 5th metatarsal tuberosity in profile; cuboid joint spaces and sinus tarsi open. (The foot lateral, p.234, is not built in S14.4 by owner decision.)
- **p.246 (knee AP):** receptor 24 × 30 cm portrait; grid / bucky if > 10 cm, non-grid if < 10 cm; digital 75 ± 5 kV (analog 65–75); supine, no pelvic rotation, leg fully extended, leg rotated internally 3°–5° so the interepicondylar line is parallel to the receptor; CR parallel to the tibial plateau: perpendicular for an average patient (ASIS-to-table 19–24 cm), 3–5° caudad if < 19 cm, 3–5° cephalad if > 24 cm; centred about 1.25 cm (½ in) distal to the patellar apex; collimate to the skin margins. Criteria: distal femur and proximal tibia / fibula; open femorotibial joint with the tibial facets seen on end; symmetric condyles and joint space (no rotation); about the medial half of the fibular head superimposed by the tibia; intercondylar eminence centred in the fossa; centre of the field at the mid-knee joint.
- **p.249 (knee lateral, mediolateral):** receptor 18 × 24 or 24 × 30 cm portrait; grid if > 10 cm; digital 75 ± 5 kV; lateral recumbent (or horizontal beam); knee flexed 20°–30°; true lateral = posterior borders of the femoral condyles superimposed, patella plane perpendicular to the receptor; CR 5°–7° cephalad (7–10° for a short patient with a wide pelvis, about 5° for a tall male with a narrow pelvis, perpendicular if the lower leg can be raised to the plane of the femur), centred about 2.5 cm (1 in) distal to the medial epicondyle; collimate to the skin margins to include femur, tibia and fibula. Criteria: patella in profile, patellofemoral and knee joints open; over-rotation = less fibular-head superimposition by the tibia, under-rotation = more; distal borders of the condyles superimposed with the cephalad angle; joint at the centre of the field.
- **Bontrager is silent (as supplied) on:** mAs, AEC, any numeric rotation tolerance (the effects of over- / under-rotation are described, not degrees), partial kV bands, any SID upper bound, numeric collimation margin or centring tolerance, the image scale, the viewer-side display of a single-limb marker, exposure appearance (`RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Knee_Foot.md` §0).

## Ankle (S14.5 Phase A; claims read by the owner and supplied in the S14.5 prompt; not re-verified by Claude Code)
- **p.239 (ankle AP):** minimum SID 102 cm; receptor 24 × 30 cm portrait; non-grid; digital 70 ± 5 kV (analog 60 ± 5); supine, legs fully extended, pillow; ankle joint centred to the CR and the receptor's long axis; dorsiflexion not forced; true AP = lower leg not rotated, the intermalleolar line NOT parallel to the receptor (lateral malleolus about 15° more posterior); CR perpendicular, midway between the malleoli; collimate to the lateral skin margins, including the distal tibia-fibula and the proximal half of the metatarsals. Criteria: distal third of tibia-fibula, both malleoli, talus, proximal half of the metatarsals, soft tissue; long axis aligned with the collimation field and the receptor; no rotation = medial mortise open and lateral mortise closed; some overlap of the distal fibula by the tibia and talus; four-sided collimation; no motion; talus penetrated (cortex and trabeculae), soft tissue visible.
- **p.242 (ankle lateral, mediolateral):** same technical factors; lateral recumbent, affected side down, knee flexed about 45°, the other leg behind, a support under the knee as needed for a true lateral; foot dorsiflexed so the sole is at a right angle to the leg, as far as tolerated, not forced; CR perpendicular to the medial malleolus; collimate the distal tibia-fibula to the mid-metatarsals. Criteria: distal third of tibia-fibula with the fibula overlapped by the tibia; talus and calcaneus in lateral profile; 5th metatarsal tuberosity, navicular and cuboid seen; no rotation = distal fibula over the posterior half of the tibia; tibiotalar joint open with a uniform space; the field includes the distal third of the lower leg, calcaneus, 5th metatarsal tuberosity and soft tissue; no motion; the lateral malleolus seen through the distal tibia and talus; soft tissue visible for effusion.
- **p.240 (AP mortise, 15°) and p.243 (AP stress): context only**, not built (S14.5-1).
- **Bontrager is silent (as supplied) on:** SID maximum, mAs, rotation / tilt tolerances in degrees, centring tolerance, collimation margins in cm, the single-limb marker display, the limit of dorsiflexion for the lateral (`RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Ankle.md` §0).

## Pelvis AP — direct reading, 2026-10-15 (S-PELVIS-REF)

This subsection has different provenance from the advisor extracts above: Codex read the owner's full PDF through Google Drive, checked title/copyright (9th ed., 2018, same ISBN), and read printed p.271–272 and p.279 directly; p.279 also visually checked. PDF page = printed page + 20 for these locators. The book stays outside Git. Facts paraphrased; no figures reused.

- **p.279:** routine AP pelvis: SID at least 102 cm; landscape IR 35 × 43 cm; grid; digital 85 ± 5 kV (analog 80 ± 5 kV, context only). Supine; arms alongside or across upper chest; head/knee supports. Align midsagittal plane to table/CR; ASIS-to-table distances equal. Internally rotate both entire lower limbs 15°–20° **unless fracture/dislocation suspected**, when movement is minimized and rotation is not attempted. CR perpendicular, midway between ASIS level and pubic symphysis (about 5 cm below ASIS); four-sided collimation; suspend respiration without a specified respiratory phase. The lower hip-routine center is outside the selected routine-pelvis scope.
- **p.279 criteria:** pelvic girdle, L5, sacrum/coccyx, femoral heads/necks and greater trochanters included; iliac wings/obturator foramina/visible ischial spines symmetric; narrowing of one obturator foramen indicates pelvic rotation toward that side. Lesser trochanters absent or tips only, greater trochanters comparable. L5/sacrum and hip borders visible through overlapping structures with pubic/ischial detail retained; sharp trabeculae/no motion. No numerical error tolerances, universal mAs or approved scoring weights derived.
- **p.271–272:** internal rotation of the **whole legs** profiles femoral necks and reduces visible lesser trochanters. Neutral/external leg rotation changes these cues. This is a separate action from rotating the pelvis. p.271 also warns against internally rotating a leg with evidence of hip fracture.
- **Shielding:** p.279 contains 2018 recommendations; recorded as source context only, not adopted as contemporary instruction or a scored rule (O86). No current shielding guidance was reviewed in this phase.

Full reference/Needs: [Pelvis AP](../RAD-LAB_Medical_Reference_Pelvis_AP.md). Every clinical claim remains provisional pending O5; unknown model/rubric values are O81–O83, image/model feasibility O84–O85.

## Shoulder Grashey — direct reading, 2026-10-15 (S-SHOULDER-REF)

Codex read printed **p.196 (PDF216)** from the owner-provided full PDF already retrieved through Drive; text and page visually checked. Same verified9th edition2018 ISBN9780323399661. Book/scans stay outside Git; facts paraphrased only.

- AP oblique for glenoid cavity (Grashey), in the nontrauma section. Erect or supine; erect may be less painful if feasible. Rotate body35°–45° toward affected side; more may be needed with rounded shoulders/recumbency to make scapula parallel to detector. Support raised shoulder/hip if recumbent. Slightly abduct flexed arm in neutral rotation; no numeric arm angles given.
- Minimum SID102cm; receptor18×24 or24×30cm landscape; grid; digital80±5kV (75–85 arithmetic interval), analog75±5kV context only. No mAs/time/EI or AEC calibration derived.
- CR perpendicular to scapulohumeral joint, about5cm inferior and5cm medial to superolateral shoulder border. Detector top about5cm above shoulder and side about5cm lateral to humerus. Upper/lateral collimation to soft-tissue margins. Suspend respiration, no phase specified.
- Criteria: glenoid in profile without humeral-head superimposition; open joint space, anterior/posterior glenoid rims superimposed; area of interest collimated; sharp trabeculae without motion; soft tissues/joint/axilla detail visible.
- No numeric error bands, weights, critical gates, universal40° optimum, pixel scale or one-image deformation model established. Historical shielding recommendation not adopted (O86). Clinical facts provisional pending O5.

## Shoulder scapular Y — direct reading, 2026-10-15 (S-SHOULDER-Y-REF)

Codex directly read text and visually checked **printed p.201/PDF221** in the owner-provided9th-edition2018 PDF (ISBN9780323399661, verified earlier). No book scans/figures/text copied to Git.

- PA oblique scapularY lateral, in shoulder-trauma section. Erect or recumbent (erect usually more comfortable); patient faces detector in anterior obliquity. Align superior scapular angle–AC articulation line perpendicular to detector; patient variation gives45°–60° body obliquity. Center scapulohumeral joint. Slightly abduct arm if possible to avoid rib overlap; do not attempt arm rotation, especially suspected fracture/dislocation.
- SID minimum102cm;24×30cm portrait; vertical grid, CR to centerline; digital80±5kV (75–85 arithmetic interval), analog75±5 context only. CR perpendicular to joint about5cm belowAC. Collimate four sides to region of interest. Suspend respiration, no phase specified.
- True lateral scapula/proximal humerus/joint; scapular body on end without rib superimposition; acromion/coracoid nearly symmetricY limbs; humeral head atY base **if not dislocated**. Sharp borders/body outline visible through proximal humerus without motion. Anterior/posterior dislocation descriptions are clinical context only, not a synthetic diagnosis/positioning rule.
- Recumbent oppositeAP oblique with injured shoulder elevated is a condition-dependent alternative, not automatically the selectedPA scope. No Neer angulation transferred. No numeric mAs/EI, grading bands, weights, scale or one-image model derived. Historical shielding not adoptedO86; clinical claims provisionalO5.
