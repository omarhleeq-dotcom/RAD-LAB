## General (technique)
- **p.3** — Chest radiograph quality depends heavily on technique. Older 50–70 kV practice hid up to about a third of the lungs behind the heart and diaphragm; 120–140 kV penetrates the mediastinum better; above 140 kV contrast falls noticeably. The air-gap technique uses a source distance of about 3–4 m.
- **p.4 (grid)** — A grid is the more common way to reduce scatter; it allows the patient to stand against the cassette and a source distance of about 2 m. The air gap is felt to give superior images.
- **p.17 (reading scheme)** — Check name and date; check the film is the right way round using the side marker; decide PA or AP; decide erect, semi-erect or supine.

## Chest PA
- **p.4** — The departmental chest radiograph is an erect PA with the patient facing the cassette.
- **p.28 (rotation)** — With correct centring the spinous processes lie midway between the medial ends of the clavicles. Rotation lengthens the clavicle-to-spinous-process distance on the side the patient is rotated *towards*; that hemithorax may look darker; the hila may look asymmetric; the sternum may project over the mediastinal contour.
- **p.29 (inspiration)** — Fewer than 6 anterior ribs above the hemidiaphragms indicates poor inspiratory effort; effects: crowded lower-zone vessels, increased lower-zone opacity, bulky-looking hila, a larger-looking heart.
- **p.32** — Slight rotation (especially to the right) can make the edge of the manubrium mimic paratracheal nodes.

## Chest LATERAL
- **p.5** — High kV reduces the need for routine laterals. The lateral shows the area anterior to the mediastinum and behind the diaphragms better; with low kV a lateral is needed to see behind the heart and the hemidiaphragms. Right-middle-lobe and lingular collapse or consolidation is better seen on the lateral.
- **p.5, Fig 2.4 (annotated lateral)** — structures labelled: substernal line, retrosternal space, trachea, aorta, left and right bronchi, posterior heart border, retrocardiac space, right and left hemidiaphragms, scapulae, skin fold from the arm.

## Chest AP
- **p.4** — Patients confined to bed or chair are imaged AP. AP is usually taken at a shorter source distance. On AP the heart is magnified (more beam divergence and a greater heart-to-detector distance); mediastinal structures may look enlarged; the clavicles project broader and overlie the apices. AP films should be interpreted with caution. **Fig 2.3** shows apparent differences in heart size and lung volume between an AP and a PA of the same patient.
- **p.17** — AP versus PA, and erect / semi-erect / supine, are judged as part of reading a film (see General).
- **p.27 (pitfalls)** — Skin-fold pseudo-pneumothorax, usually on AP films with the patient lying or sitting against the cassette (an ICU semi-erect example).
- No numeric AP source distance is given in the notes.
